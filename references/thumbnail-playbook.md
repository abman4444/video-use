# Thumbnail Playbook

Distilled from Marcus Jones, "The New Way to Make Thumbnails (2026)"
(https://youtu.be/-8AkvDSZ1cg, 19:16, published 2026-10-04). His stated track
record: 2,000+ thumbnails, ~64M views. Notes are paraphrased; specific values
are his channel's results, not universal constants.

Source quality: the spoken points come from the full transcript. Details marked
**(on screen)** were read off the video by an AI visual walkthrough (vidIQ) and
were not spoken — treat exact strings and hex codes there as likely but
unverified.

## The core idea: work in this order

1. **Concept** — what the thumbnail shows and why anyone would care.
2. **Audience psychology** — believability, relatability, text.
3. **Design** — fonts, colors, spacing, polish.

Most creators spend their time on step 3. The leverage is in steps 1 and 2: a
beautifully designed thumbnail of a concept nobody wants still loses.

His analogy: gold only exists in small pockets. Pick a random spot and dig hard,
and you'll usually find nothing — not because you dig badly, but because most
ground is empty. Most thumbnail concepts are empty ground. Find where the gold
already is before you dig.

## 1. Concept — base it on proven outliers

- Every thumbnail should be inspired by one or a combination of high-performing
  videos. Where to look:
  - **Small channels** (low thousands to low tens of thousands of subs) whose
    video got hundreds of thousands of views.
  - **Mid-size channels** whose video blew up into one of the top performers in
    the niche for that period.
- Search **your niche and adjacent niches**. Far-away niches (e.g. Minecraft →
  education) can work, but it takes experience to know which elements transfer.
- **Never copy directly.** Viewers either recognize the copy, or their brain
  assumes "I've seen this video" and skips it. Take the *pattern/framework*, not
  the pixels, colors, or layout.
- The result must feel **fresh** — a new twist on a proven pattern. If the style
  has been done many times in the niche, it won't stand out.
- **(on screen)** Bad vs good examples: "The 7 Levels of Rapper" (4.1M) copied
  1:1 as "The 7 Levels of Humour" (330K) — same rainbow 1–7 bars. Versus
  combining two proven ideas ("THEY'RE LYING TO YOU" taped-mouth faces + a
  "guru scam" debunk) into an original "Don't Listen" thumbnail (163K).
- Research tools: an outlier tracker (he uses his own, Velio — outlier
  multipliers vs channel average) or plain YouTube search (free, noisier).
  vidIQ's outliers search does the same job.

## 2. Audience psychology

### Believability
- Show something extreme enough to make a viewer stop — but not so extreme they
  conclude "no way, clickbait."
- Failed example: "IN 2 MINS" → 952K views. Nobody believes it.
- Hidden-math trap: a "952K views — 96% more than usual" card implies the
  channel normally gets ~500K. Viewers do that math subconsciously.
- Analogy: a free-kick arrow that curves realistically is believable; a
  zig-zag scribble isn't.
- **(on screen)** No elements should contradict each other.

### Relatability
- Viewers must see themselves in the thumbnail, especially in "before → after"
  transformation content.
- His winner (5.2M views): **Views 43 ↓ → Views 107K ↑**. 43 views matches
  where his audience actually is; 107K is exciting *and* imaginable. An amateur
  would have put 10M on the right and lost the click.
- Same thumbnail showed a setting the audience actually uses (related video on
  Shorts) vs. the contrarian change (related video: none). Viewers recognize
  their own behavior and wonder if changing it is the fix.
- Counter-examples: "where to invest your $1M" for an audience without $1M; a
  fitness "before" photo that looks nothing like the viewer.
- **(on screen)** His designers' brief names the target viewer concretely:
  a 21–30-year-old US male with under 200 subs. Write down yours.

### Text
- Thumbnail words must be **borrowed or adapted from proven outliers**, never
  guessed or used to fill space.
- Keep a swipe file of outlier thumbnails with psychologically strong text.
  His examples **(stats on screen)**: "The old Instagram is gone" (115K vs 18K
  avg, ~6x), "GET OBSESSED" (~176x outlier), "it just works" (~103x).
- Adapt the *structure*: "The old Instagram is gone" → "The old YouTube is
  gone." What works in his niche may not work in yours; the method transfers.

## 3. Design

- **Standardize what you've tested.** Run the same element (font, a shade of
  gray, a shade of blue) across several videos, keep the winner, make it your
  standard. His gray #282828 beat three other grays on his channel.
- **(on screen)** His brand sheet:
  - Fonts: Roboto (anything mimicking YouTube UI/stats), Akrobat Black in all
    caps (bold headlines), Caveat Bold (handwritten/casual).
  - Colors: #282828 dark gray (UI backgrounds), #ababab light gray (secondary
    UI text), #00c0ff blue (view graphs), #20cd1a green (positive metrics,
    up-arrows, "winning" borders).
- **Primary text: 5 words max.** Supporting text (timestamps, small UI labels)
  doesn't count, but primary text over five words gets fewer clicks in most
  niches.
- **80px safe zone** on a 1920×1080 canvas. Primary elements (headline, key
  number/card, main arrow, the face — especially the eyes) stay at least 80px
  from every edge. Background details, minor labels, the top of the hair can
  cross it. Thumbnails that ignore this feel cramped.
- **(on screen)** Delivery spec: 1920×1080 JPG under 2 MB.

## AI-assisted workflow (his Pikzels demo — tool-agnostic)

1. Find an outlier concept; paste its URL into a thumbnail tool's "recreate"
   mode (Pikzels; ChatGPT-style image models also work).
2. Swap in yourself via a saved persona (built from 3 photos of your face).
   Optionally attach a reference image for a specific object (e.g. an exact
   car).
3. Generate a batch (he uses 8) to get a canvas to work from — not a final.
4. Edit with **very specific** instructions. Not "change this to views" but
   "change 'Estimated revenue' to 'Views' and the number from $11,018.84 to
   107.8k." Name what to keep: "keep the style, colors, look and feel exactly
   the same."
5. Re-fit the psychology: he changed a flat revenue graph to a views graph that
   starts low and climbs, because his audience wants growth from a low base.
6. Apply the design rules (background shade, font, padding).
7. Final pass: "upscale to high-res" and re-assert the persona to clean up the
   face.

His stance on AI: fine as a tool you drive (doing what you'd do in
Photoshop/Canva, faster); not fine as the creator. It narrows the gap with big
channels that have designers and budgets.

**(on screen)** His final thumbnail: him on the right looking at camera; a hand
holding a phone with dark-mode YouTube Studio (#282828); a "Views 101.7k" badge
with a green up-arrow; a rising cyan line graph; dark textured background.

## Yes-only checklist (every answer must be yes before it ships)

- [ ] **Proven** — based on researched outlier(s), with the inspiration saved?
- [ ] **Fresh** — a genuine twist, not a copy?
- [ ] **Believable** — coherent story, nothing contradictory, not so
      exaggerated it reads as a lie?
- [ ] **Copy** — primary text ≤ 5 words, adapted from proven phrases?
- [ ] **Audience fit** — would your specific target viewer see themselves in
      the stats/copy and think "this is for me"?
- [ ] **Brand** — approved fonts and colors only?
- [ ] **Canvas** — 1920×1080, key elements inside the 80px safe zone, file
      under 2 MB?

## Then test

Make several variants and A/B test them (YouTube's Test & Compare). Every test
should feed the brand sheet and the swipe file. He also pitches Pikzels
Intelligence (beta, connects to your channel, re-packages underperforming
videos and auto-tests them with your approval) — optional.

## Caveats

- He is a Pikzels affiliate and friends with its founder; he also promotes his
  own research tool. Neither is required.
- The hex codes, fonts, and the "43 → 107K" numbers fit *his* audience of
  small YouTubers. Copy the method; find your own values by testing.
